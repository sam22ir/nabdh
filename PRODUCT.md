# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Nabdh serves two primary authenticated roles in Algeria.

Patients use the app to find private-practice doctors, compare specialties, locations, availability, reviews, and book appointments for themselves or a Profile under their account. They are often mobile-first, Arabic-first, time-constrained, and may be making decisions while stressed or helping a family member.

Doctors use the app to manage their public profile, clinics, schedules, appointments, and patient-facing trust signals. They need the interface to support professional control without adding administrative friction.

French support matters, but Arabic RTL is the primary experience. Clinic Admin and Super Admin are future/internal roles and should not dominate the current product design.

## Product Purpose

Nabdh is a medical appointment booking product that connects Algerian patients with private-practice doctors. It should make the next useful action obvious: find a doctor, understand whether they are appropriate, choose a clinic and time, book an appointment, and manage the appointment lifecycle.

Success means fewer phone calls, less uncertainty about availability, clearer doctor profiles, faster booking, and calmer appointment management for both patients and doctors.

## Brand Personality

Calm, official, fast.

The product voice should feel respectful, precise, and institution-grade without becoming cold or bureaucratic. It should reduce anxiety, keep choices legible, and move users through medical workflows with confidence.

## Anti-references

Do not make Nabdh look like a generic blue hospital app.

Avoid childish emoji onboarding, decorative medical illustrations, oversized rounded cards, soft SaaS dashboard styling, vague wellness-app warmth, and visual clutter that makes medical actions feel less serious.

Do not use decoration to imply trust. Trust should come from clear profile data, availability, verification context, appointment status, clinic location, and predictable interaction patterns.

## Design Principles

Make the next medical action obvious.

Use official calm, not ornamental healthcare softness. The interface should feel credible enough for medical decisions and light enough for repeated mobile use.

Design Arabic first. RTL layout, Arabic copy rhythm, line lengths, icon placement, and touch flow are primary, with French treated as a real supported language rather than an afterthought.

Speed is a product quality. Reduce scanning time, avoid unnecessary steps, prefer inline progressive flows, and keep appointment actions close to the context that explains them.

Trust is contextual. Verification, reviews, availability, clinic information, and status labels should be specific and understandable instead of decorative badges.

Keep patient and doctor surfaces related but not identical. Patients need discovery and reassurance; doctors need schedule control, appointment triage, and profile completeness.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for text and controls. Keep touch targets large enough for mobile use, support reduced motion, and never rely on color alone for appointment status, verification, errors, or warnings.

Design for Arabic-first RTL and French localization, including longer translated strings and text wrapping. Forms and appointment flows should remain clear for users under time pressure, users with low digital confidence, and users on smaller Android devices.
